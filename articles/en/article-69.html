The $730 Billion AI Gamble: The Hidden Computing Power War from Billion-Dollar Deals to HBM Supply Chain Bottlenecks

Trillions in capital shift from model training to underlying infrastructure, reshaping the global AI landscape through compute and energy.

Where is the $730 Billion Going? The Fundamental Shift from Model Frenzy to Compute Infrastructure

In 2026, the global AI industry is experiencing an unprecedented wave of capital. Estimates show that the four major North American tech giants will collectively invest over $730 billion in AI in 2026, while total global enterprise AI investment has reached 4.2 trillion yuan. In this 'most expensive gamble in the history of civilization,' the flow of funds has shifted significantly. Over the past two years, capital狂热ly chased the software layer of large models, but the market is no longer willing to pay for mere 'chatbots.' Funds are accelerating towards underlying hardware and compute infrastructure. GPUs, optical modules, advanced packaging, and custom chips have become the main battlegrounds. To support these massive expenditures, tech giants are even issuing large-scale debt, with the top six giants alone issuing $240 billion in bonds in 2026. Behind this shift lies a renewed understanding of AI's underlying logic: without a massive compute foundation, any stunning model capability is just a castle in the air. As model parameters grow exponentially, compute has replaced algorithms as the primary productive force determining the survival of AI enterprises.

Frequent Billion-Dollar Compute Deals: AI Giants Locking in Long-Term 'Rations'

Facing compute hunger, AI giants are no longer satisfied with scattered chip purchases, but are turning to long-term, massive compute locking. Recently, Anthropic signed a $10 billion, 6-year compute contract with AI infrastructure unicorn Volta, and the two will jointly build an AI factory in Norway equipped with Nvidia's Vera Rubin system. Founded just 7 months ago, Volta has attracted investment from giants like Nvidia and Dell, reaching a valuation of $2.4 billion, with its core model integrating capital, power, land, and chips. Meanwhile, Elon Musk announced that SpaceX will permanently and exclusively adopt Nvidia GPUs, even planning to build a 10-gigawatt space AI compute cluster. These billion-dollar deals indicate that compute has become the 'strategic oil' of the AI era. For leading large model developers, whoever locks in stable compute supply for the next few years in advance will take the lead in fierce model iterations. Compute suppliers are also evolving from simple equipment sellers into comprehensive infrastructure operators.

Explosion in Token Usage and Inference: Optical Interconnects and HBM as Core Bottlenecks

As AI evolves from 'chatting' to 'doing things' in the L3 agent era, the structure of compute demand is undergoing dramatic changes. Data shows that China's daily Token usage has surged from 100 billion in early 2024 to 140 trillion in early 2026, a thousand-fold increase in two years. DeepSeek's daily Token processing volume has reached a staggering 8 trillion. This means the重心 of compute consumption has fully shifted from pre-training to inference services. On the inference side, the demand for high concurrency and low latency has made optical interconnects and HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) core bottlenecks. Goldman Sachs recently significantly raised its target prices for optical module manufacturers, pointing out that the ratio of AI chips to optical modules is continuously increasing, and optical interconnects will become the core support for compute growth. Meanwhile, HBM's capacity constraints directly limit the shipment of high-end GPUs, with supply chain yield and capacity becoming choke point challenges. In the compute hidden war, solving the memory wall and communication wall has become a critical battle as important as developing core chips.

Energy Crisis and Geopolitical Gaming: The Hidden Ceiling of Compute Expansion

The rapid surge in compute is crashing into the hidden ceilings of the physical world and geopolitics. The energy consumption of AI data centers is rising exponentially, triggering energy crises in many regions. Texas, USA, has announced a suspension of data center grid connection approvals, requiring comprehensive audits; public and political opposition to AI expansion crowding out residential power is growing. Compute is not just a technical issue, but a national-level strategic game. The enforcement of the EU AI Act and the tightening of data cross-border and compute control by various countries are creating heavy regulatory hurdles for the global layout of tech giants. Additionally, the unilateral North American bet is shifting towards a balanced global layout, with the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and other regions rushing to build compute centers. In this gamble, power acquisition capabilities, geopolitical policy risks, and supply chain autonomy have become barriers harder to cross than capital. The main factors limiting AI companies from building dedicated compute are shifting from demand and chips to power and compliance.

The Dawn of the L3 Agent Era: Trillions in Capital Betting on Commercialization in Real-World Scenarios

The $730 billion investment ultimately requires a closed-loop of commercialization to sustain it. Currently, the AI industry has unquestionably entered the L3 agent era, where AI can not only think but also take action. The performance gap between leading Chinese and US models has substantially narrowed, and the focus of competition has shifted from 'leaderboard brushing' to commercialization in real-world scenarios. In 2025, generative AI private investment reached 1.2 trillion yuan, with capital accelerating into physical world applications like embodied intelligence, industrial AI, and autonomous driving. The landing of Chinese large models is mainly concentrated in scenarios like the Internet, finance, and industrial manufacturing. However, the compute infrastructure construction cycle is as long as three to five years, while the market's patience for commercialization is only one to two years; this time gap constitutes the biggest realization risk. The listing of embodied intelligence companies like Unitree on the capital market marks AI's move from the digital world to physical entities. The ultimate return on trillions in capital depends on whether AI can create irreplaceable real productivity across thousands of industries, completing the leap from a 'scientific gamble' to an 'industrial cornerstone'.
