01.AI Exits the 'Big Model Six Tigers': China's AI Startup Capital Logic Shifts to Profitability Validation

Bidding Farewell to Burning Money for Scale: Benchmarking Palantir, Kai-Fu Lee's B2B Pivot Reflects New Survival Rules in the Second Half of the AI Era

In July 2026, the AI industry witnessed a notable narrative shift. 01.AI, founded by Kai-Fu Lee, officially updated its external positioning, explicitly refusing to be categorized among the “Big Model Six Tigers.” This move was not merely a PR exercise but a complete strategic pivot. Since the second half of 2025, Lee has established a unified external narrative: the entrepreneurial window for the foundational model track has closed, and 01.AI will fully benchmark against US data analytics giant Palantir, shifting its business focus to the ToB (enterprise) market. In recent product launches, 01.AI introduced “Wance,” a product aimed at enterprise decision-makers, emphasizing that AI transformation must be driven by the company’s top leadership, with the core being the construction of competitive barriers through closed-loop data. Lee himself has taken the lead as the core salesperson. This transition from the “parameter wars, benchmark wars” of the foundational model track to the “deployment wars, revenue wars” of the enterprise service track marks the collective departure of China’s large model startups from their blindly tech-obsessed adolescence into a pragmatic adulthood. Shedding the industry classification label is essentially 01.AI’s attempt to break free from a purely technical comparison system and reshape its new capital market identity as a “pioneer of AI commercialization.”

The transformation of 01.AI reflects a profound restructuring of the capital logic behind China’s large model startups. Over the past two years, the domestic AI circle has been dominated by a “computing power subsidies for user scale” burn-rate model, attempting to capture market share through low-cost or even free API interfaces. However, entering 2026, capital markets have completely lost patience with this model that lacks self-sustaining capabilities. According to media reports, mere DAU data and API call volumes can no longer support inflated valuation bubbles, and capital has shifted its focus to the quality of commercial deployment. Investor attention is turning to AI entities with clear profitability paths. 01.AI’s benchmark, Palantir, delivered stellar results in Q1 2026 with revenue of $1.63 billion (up 85% YoY) and GAAP net profit surging over 300%, with a market cap approaching $300 billion. This provides a compelling benchmark for domestic AI entrepreneurs: in the second half of AI, software service providers that can deeply integrate into enterprise core business flows and achieve stable profitability are far more investable than “model factories” that simply stack parameters. Investors are beginning to realize that technological innovation without a business closed-loop ultimately remains an expensive toy in the lab.

The moves of global tech giants are also providing footnotes for this shift in capital logic. In July 2026, Apple’s market cap broke through the $5 trillion mark for the first time intraday. Unlike its peers’ massive AI infrastructure CapEx of hundreds of billions — for instance, Alphabet announced raising its annual CapEx to up to $205 billion, triggering market panic — Apple has maintained a rare “restraint” in AI spending. Apple has not blindly built massive data centers but instead prefers leasing computing power, cleverly embedding its AI functions into the existing hardware and services flywheel. The market voted with real money, proving that this shareholder-friendly strategy of avoiding the “CapEx trap” is becoming the most sought-after safe-haven attribute. Meanwhile, profitability is also emerging for overseas AI unicorns. According to foreign media, Anthropic’s annualized revenue is estimated to have reached $30 billion, with enterprise and coding agents as core growth engines, approaching its first profitability milestone, with AI product-market fit (PMF) formally established. Whether it’s Apple’s “no burn” strategy or Anthropic’s commercialization validation, both are sending a clear signal to the Chinese market: the endgame of AI is not a bottomless computing power arms race, but whether it can create irreplaceable value in real business scenarios.

Facing the shift in capital logic, Chinese AI companies are exploring a dual-wheel-driven path of “open-source ecosystem building, ToB profit-seeking.” On one hand, Chinese large models are leveraging their “low cost + fine-tunable” advantages to penetrate global markets. Chip architecture expert Jim Keller publicly praised Chinese large models for reducing costs by 5x; Moonshot AI officially open-sourced Kimi K3 with 2.8 trillion parameters, and Meituan also open-sourced a 1.6 trillion parameter model trained entirely on domestic computing power. This open-sharing model not only reduces the marginal cost of AI deployment across industries but also helps Chinese companies accumulate a vast developer ecosystem. On the other hand, at the ToB deployment level, Chinese AI companies are accelerating their evolution from “general-purpose large models” to “industry-specific agents.” From Deta Intelligence, a humanoid robot foundation model company completing six funding rounds in half a year, to Kuaishou releasing its flagship Agentic Coding programming model, agent technology deployment is penetrating deep into enterprise core business operations. Chinese companies are leveraging mature open-source foundations, combined with private deployment and closed-loop data, to build cost-effective industry solutions. This “open-source foundation + commercialized upper layer” approach avoids head-on confrontation with global giants on general-purpose computing power while precisely targeting the rigid demand for digital transformation in the real economy.

From the fading of the “Big Model Six Tigers” label to the firm pivot toward benchmarking Palantir, 01.AI’s choice is a microcosm of China’s AI industry maturing. In 2026, the AI track has completely shed its initial frenzy and restlessness, ushering in a cruel but necessary business baptism. Capital markets no longer blindly pay for the ethereal “AGI vision” but strictly demand to see real revenue growth and profit statements. For Chinese large model startups, underlying technological leadership is certainly important, but whether they can take root across thousands of industries and achieve self-sustaining capability in fierce market competition is what determines survival. Bidding farewell to the old era of burning money for scale and embracing the new era of commercialization and profitability validation, China’s AI industry is moving toward true strength through pragmatism. Surviving and making money — that’s the hardest truth of the second half of AI.
