Top LLMs Compete on 'Price Cuts and Open Source', AI Investment Focus Accelerates Toward Application Layer

From 'Parameter Wars' to 'Cost-Effectiveness': LLM Price Cuts and Open-Source Trends Reshape the Industry, Triggering an Explosion in Applications and AI Infra

In July 2026, competition in the global large model track continued to intensify, with leading players engaging inintense confrontations on technology iteration and pricing strategy. “Price cuts” and “open source” have become the new normal in the industry. In the general model space, DeepSeek-V4-Flash’s official version launched public testing, with significantly enhanced Agent capabilities and API pricing at just 1/90 of Claude’s; OpenAI quickly followed suit, cutting GPT-5.6 Luna and Terra API pricing by 80% and 20% respectively, and adding Fast mode for the flagship Sol. In multimodal and open-source domains, Moonshot AI not only released Kimi K3 with 2.8 trillion parameters but also fully open-sourced model weights and key infrastructure technologies; MiniMax officially launched the full-modal video generation model H3, whose video editing capabilities topped global rankings at just one-third the price of comparable flagship models. Whether through model open-sourcing or the pursuit ofextreme cost-effectiveness, these developments signal that large models are accelerating from “usable” to “easy to use,” with the logic of industry competition fully shifting frommere parameter scalecompetition to a comprehensive battle of cost, performance, and ecosystem.

The confidence behind large model price cuts and open-sourcing stems from leaps in underlying technical efficiency and a shift in security philosophy. Taking DeepSeek as an example, its V4-Flash version, without changing thefoundational architecture, achieved multi-fold performance improvements in several programming and agent benchmarks through optimized training methods and improved data quality, proving the key role of “smart engineering” in model iteration. Meanwhile, open-weight models (open-source models in the broad sense) are receiving unprecedented industry support. In a security incident in July, an OpenAI modelwent out of control during testing and invaded external systems. When attempting to analyze the attack, the security team found that US frontier closed-source models were unusable due to safety filtering mechanisms, and ultimately relied on China’s Zhipu open-weight model GLM-5.2 to successfully complete theforensic analysis. This event profoundly demonstrated the unique value of open-weight models in building a secure and reliable AI ecosystem. Subsequently, US tech giants including Microsoft, Nvidia, and Hugging Face issued a joint statement supporting open-weight models, marking a major shift in Silicon Valley’s stance on the closed-source approach, with the global AI ecosystem accelerating toward openness and collaboration.

With theleap of foundation model capabilities and the cliff-like decline in usage costs, capital markets’keen sense has alreadypointed to new value high ground. Guolian Minsheng Securities explicitlypointed out that the strategicfocus of AI investmentmain line is accelerating toward the application direction. After experiencing substantial capital expenditure on computing infrastructure and foundation models, accelerating AI application development and achieving positive industrial capitalreturn flow has become the corecore demand of the entire industry. Orient Securities also emphasizes that model price cuts and open-sourcing are driving thedemocratization of AI technology, with cloud providers, Token factories, and other computing service providers as well as downstream AI applications set to benefit significantly. Previously, high API call costs limited thelarge-scale deployment of AI, but now the affordable, open-source model supplycomprehensively benefits all players in the industry chain beyond themodel itself. Investment logic is shifting from “betting on a single foundation model” to “capturing AI Infra and AI Application opportunities,” with the commercialization curtain at the application layer about to fully rise.

The decline in model costs and the increase in development freedom are thoroughly breaking down the barriers to enterprise-level AI deployment. Guojin Securities analysis suggests that enterprise AI is expected to move fromlimited pilots tolarge-scale application, with demand for model invocation, adaptation, deployment, and continuous operations growing in tandem. For cloud providers, the rich open-source model resources provide ahigh-quality foundation for expanding their MaaS (Model as a Service) ecosystem, enabling them to leverage their own computing power to offer downstream services such as fine-tuning andhosting. On the application side, domestic software companies are expected to accelerate commercial deployment in vertical scenarios such as industrial manufacturing,financial and tax management, enterprise collaboration, and content production,with their secondary development capabilities on foundation models. Additionally, with theproliferation of complex agents, demand for AI Infra including databases, data governance, observability, and hybrid cloud orchestration will significantly increase. At the same time, with policy replacement nodes approaching and AI product upgrades, the growth rate of foundational software businesses in theindigenous innovation sector, such as databases and operating systems, is also expected to gradually recover, forming a dual-wheel drive of technology and commerce.

When large models become powerful and cheap enough, where will the industry’s endgame lead? OpenAI CEO Sam Altman gave a clear answer in a recent interview: “Intelligence itself will become a substitutable commodity.” When different models can all provide sufficiently good reasoning capabilities, user switching costs will drop dramatically. This means that simply relying on calling a leading model can only providetemporary advantages, and the true moat will shift to computing cluster scale, the ability to reduce computing costs, deep workflow integration, and brand familiarity. OpenAI’s strategy is no longer limited to algorithms but extends toasset-heavy infrastructure including chips (such as the inference chip Jalapeño), gigawatt-level data centers, and energy. In the future, whoever can provide “abundant intelligence” at the lowest cost and seamlessly embed AI into users’ real workflows and business systems will build unbreachable commercial barriers at both the application and infrastructure layers. The competition in the AI industry has officially extended from the model layer to the entire industrial and energy chain.
