Chinese LLMs Sweep Top 5 on Global Routing Platforms: Open-Source Ecosystem Reshapes the Global AI Landscape

From dominating the OpenRouter leaderboard to trillion-parameter open-weights, China's AI is breaking closed-source monopolies and leading a new era of global AI inclusivity with extreme cost-effectiveness and efficient computing infrastructure.

In July 2026, the global AI industry reached a historic moment. According to public data from the neutral large model routing platform OpenRouter, as of July 27, the top five daily Token consumption rankings on the platform were dominated by Chinese manufacturers for the first time. Among them, Xiaomi MiMo-V2.5 topped the chart with 1.6 trillion Tokens processed per day; DeepSeek V4 Flash, Tencent Hunyuan Hy3, DeepSeek V4 Pro, and Zhipu GLM-5.2 followed closely, each with daily processing volumes reaching hundreds of billions. By comparison, Nvidia’s Nemotron 3 Ultra was the highest-ranked non-Chinese model that day, while Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 5 ranked only 11th, and Google’s Gemini 3 Flash Preview ranked 12th. This data not only set a new historical record on OpenRouter but also reflects US companies “voting with their feet.” CNBC reported that since February, the peak proportion of Tokens from Chinese models used by US companies has reached 46%, far exceeding the previous average of 11%. Chinese large models, with superior performance and highly competitive costs, have established an undeniable dominant position in the global developer ecosystem, marking a substantial shift in the center of gravity of global AI applications. Notably, this is not an accidental traffic fluctuation but a concentrated manifestation of the full maturity of China’s AI industry chain in model capability, engineering optimization, and commercial deployment, completely rewriting the global large model traffic distribution pattern long dominated by US tech giants.

Behind the dominance of Chinese models on the leaderboard lies the prosperity of the open-source ecosystem and the extreme cost-effectiveness of the “Chinese solution.” Recently, Moonshot AI officially open-sourced the Kimi K3 model with 2.8 trillion parameters, adopting a MoE architecture with support for 1 million Token context, with API pricing only one-third that of Western closed-source flagships; Alibaba also announced it would open-source Qwen3.8 with 2.4 trillion parameters. These trillion-parameter models do not rely solely on parameter stacking but have improved computing efficiency by 250% through self-developed computing architectures. In terms of pricing strategy, Chinese open-source models are typically 60% to 90% cheaper than Western closed-source flagship products, with some models’ input costs being over 30 times cheaper. Hugging Face data shows that over the past year, Chinese model downloads accounted for 41% of the total, exceeding 10 billion cumulative downloads. This “high-spec, low-price” strategy with permissive open-source licenses (such as MIT, Apache 2.0) has directly broken the technology oligopoly of a few companies, enabling global developers and grassroots innovators to access cutting-edge AI capabilities at low cost, truly realizing the global sharing of technological dividends. Additionally, domestic platforms like AtomGit have attracted over 11 million registered users, forming a massive developer community with internal and external synergy. This ecological flywheel based on open-source co-construction is amplifying the global influence and voice of Chinese models at a snowballing pace.

While parameter scales are skyrocketing, the global AI race is accelerating toward a new watershed of “efficiency” and “architecture.” San Francisco AI lab Poolside recently released the 118B parameter model Laguna S 2.1, which defeated behemoths like DeepSeek with 1.6 trillion parameters in programming agent tasks. Its core secret lies in the sparse MoE architecture, which only activates 8B parameters during inference, maintaining knowledge capacity while significantly compressing inference costs. Meanwhile, OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 also explicitly emphasizes finding a balance between intelligence and cost, reducing Token consumption by optimizing inference engines and Agent workflows. This indicates that the crude “parameter war” and “computing power war” model has hit a ceiling. Tokenizer efficiency, inference cost optimization, and the ability to deploy in real business scenarios are becoming the new yardsticks for measuring the true value of large models. The competition in AI programming and applications is shifting from “buying more GPUs” to “making every GPU do more work,” with architectural innovation and engineering optimization becoming key to determining victory in the second half. For enterprise users, in complex Agent tasks with reasoning mode enabled, a single task can consume hundreds of thousands of Tokens, so even small improvements in Tokenizer efficiency can save enterprises huge computing bills — this is the most direct commercial value brought by the efficiency revolution.

The prosperity of large models cannot be separated from the support of underlying computing infrastructure and the validation of commercial deployment. On the policy and infrastructure front, China has officially launched a special action to enhance IPv6 capabilities for AI large models. Hangzhou is taking a forward-looking approach to building computing networks andnew power grids, while Ningxia Zhongwei is advancing “computing-electricity synergy” with green power directly supplied to data centers, laying a solid network and energy foundation for the intelligent era. On the industry front, the capital direction has undergone profound adjustments — the “computing power subsidies for scale” model is no longer favored, and the quality of commercial deployment has become the core consideration. Taking 01.AI as an example, it has explicitly benchmarked against Palantir and fully pivoted to ToB business, pursuing the goal of becoming the first AI company to achieve stable profitability. As computing infrastructure continues to improve and enterprises find clear business closed-loop, China’s AI industry is forming a dual-wheel-driven pattern of “new infrastructure + commercialization.” This full-chain integration from underlying hardware to upper-layer applications provides a continuous stream of commercial blood and a solid foundation for the iterative evolution and global expansion of domestic large models. It can be foreseen that with the promotion of green computing models like “computing-electricity synergy,” China’s AI industry will reduce carbon emissions while further enhancing its comprehensive competitiveness in the global computing market, achieving a win-win outcome for both economic benefits and social responsibility.

As AI capabilities surge exponentially, security governance and the open-source mission have become a double-edged sword reshaping the global landscape. Recently, over 1,100 AI researchers from leading institutions including OpenAI and Google jointly called for the establishment of an international mechanism to safely manage the development of frontier AI, while AI Agent security incidents have also sounded the alarm for the industry. Against this backdrop, the open-source strategy of Chinese large models demonstrates a more profound global significance. China’s trillion-parameter open-source models are committed to bridging the global “intelligence divide,” providing advanced and affordable computing tools for underdeveloped countries and grassroots developers. By breaking down the technical barriers imposed by closed-source approaches, China’s open-source ecosystem not only provides a transparent and controllable new paradigm for global AI governance but also substantially reshapes the underlying logic and future landscape of global technology competition in the process of promoting the inclusive development of AI, making AI truly a universal infrastructure that benefits all of humanity. Facing increasingly complex AI security challenges, China is pooling global wisdom through open-source communities to jointly improve model evaluation and security monitoring standards. This open and inclusive attitude has not only won widespread recognition from the international developer community but has also contributed Chinese solutions to building a more fair and reasonable global AI governance system.
